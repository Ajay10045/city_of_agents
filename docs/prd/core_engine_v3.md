## 0. Design Pillars

1. **One Source of Truth.** The LLM-generated City Profile is the root. It defines every distribution, every structural index, every cultural norm. Change the city, and you get an entirely different political universe.
2. **Math First, Narrative Second.** Every mechanic runs on deterministic formulas seeded from the profile. LLMs dramatize the results — they never *decide* them. Two runs on the same profile with the same PRNG seed yield identical numbers.
3. **The Gap Is the Game.** The central tension: *You promised X. The city delivered 0.6X. Where did the rest go?* The answer is always traceable to specific minister traits and city structural weaknesses.
4. **Persona-Driven Agents.** Every citizen is an LLM character. Their demographic profile, personality, and capability traits compose a unique prompt template — so when the LLM speaks as Ramesh the rickshaw driver or Priya the finance minister, it reflects who they *are*, not a generic response.
5. **Visible vs. Silent.** The politically active citizens dominate the narrative between turns. The silent majority just… votes. This gap between perceived and actual approval is where election surprises come from.

## 1. The City Profile (Root of Everything)

The City Profile is NOT a random seed. It is a **structured document generated by an LLM** — using web research, real-world data, and domain knowledge about the target city. Once generated and validated, everything downstream is deterministic.

### 1.1 How the Profile Is Created

```
Input:  City name + optional player constraints
        (e.g., "New Delhi", or "A fictional South Asian megacity with high corruption")

Process: LLM generates a structured JSON profile using web search,
         real-world analogs, and domain knowledge.

Output:  A validated JSON document conforming to the City Profile Schema.

Post-generation: A PRNG is seeded from a hash of the profile.
                 This PRNG drives all stochastic elements (event rolls,
                 corruption noise, agent sampling variance) ensuring
                 reproducibility from profile alone.
```

### 1.3 Validation Rules

After LLM generation, the profile is validated programmatically:

| Rule | Check |
|---|---|
| All `city_parameters` values are 0–100 | Range check |
| All demographic `percent` fields sum to 100 within each distribution | Sum check |
| `personality_climate` means are 0–100 | Range check |
| At least 3 `media_outlets` present | Count check |
| `dominant_religions` matches `religion_distribution` | Consistency check |
| `election_turn` < `total_turns` | Logic check |
| `minister_count` < `agent_count` | Logic check |
| `budget.max_debt` > 0 | Positive check |
| `budget.interest_rate` in (0, 0.20) | Range check |
| `communal_config.tension_baseline` 0–100 | Range check |

If validation fails, the LLM is re-prompted with specific errors to fix.

## 3. The Citizen (Universal Agent Template)

Every person in the simulation — minister, opposition leader, shopkeeper — follows this exact schema. The schema is designed as an **LLM persona template**: every attribute can be injected into a prompt like *"You are a 28-year-old Hindu auto-rickshaw driver in urban Ranpur..."*.

### 3.1 Demographic Profile

Sampled from the city profile's demographic distributions. These define **who the citizen is** in the city's social fabric — their exposure to different city systems and their sensitivity to changes.

| Attribute | Type | Sampled From | Persona Prompt Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Age Group** | Categorical | `age_distribution` | Shapes voice, priorities, patience |
| **Income Bracket** | Categorical¹ | `income_distribution` | Determines economic sensitivity and policy exposure |
| **Religion** | Categorical | `religion_distribution` | Identity group affiliation, communal tension sensitivity, sensitivity multipliers |
| **Profession** | Categorical | `profession_distribution` | What city systems they depend on daily |
| **Education Level** | 0–100 | `education_distribution` × income correlation | Political awareness, media literacy, narrative sophistication |
| **Location** | Categorical | `location_distribution` | Physical exposure to infrastructure, pollution, policing |

¹ Income is sampled as a 0–100 percentile, then bucketed: Poor (0–25), Lower-Mid (25–45), Middle (45–70), Upper-Mid (70–90), Rich (90–100).

> **Example persona fragment:** *"Ramesh Yadav, 28, Hindu, auto-rickshaw driver, income percentile 32 (Poor bracket), education level 30, lives in the Urban Core of Ranpur."*

### 3.2 Personality Traits

Generated from the city profile's `personality_climate` means with individual variance (`Normal(mean, σ=15), clamped 0–100`). These are **behavioral tendencies** — they shape how the citizen *reacts* to what happens, not their skills.

| Trait | Range | Derived From | What It Governs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Integrity** | 0–100 | `integrity_mean ± σ` | Resistance to corruption; sensitivity to corruption scandals |
| **Empathy** | 0–100 | `empathy_mean ± σ` | Concern for others' suffering; reaction to inequality |
| **Risk Appetite** | 0–100 | `risk_appetite_mean ± σ`, age-correlated | Tolerance for disruptive/bold policies; volatility of sentiment |
| **Ambition** | 0–100 | `ambition_mean ± σ` | Drive for status; shapes minister advice toward big, visible projects |
| **Conscientiousness** | 0–100 | `conscientiousness_mean ± σ` | Work ethic, follow-through; affects execution quality when minister |
| **Authority Respect** | 0–100 | `authority_respect_mean ± σ` | Deference to government vs. anti-establishment instinct |
| **Corruption Tolerance** | 0–100 | `corruption_tolerance_mean ± σ` | Desensitization to graft; high = "everyone does it, whatever" |

**Personality-Demographic Correlations** (applied as adjustments after sampling):

| Demographic | Trait Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Age 18–25 | Risk Appetite +8, Authority Respect -5 |
| Age 65+ | Risk Appetite -10, Authority Respect +8 |
| Profession = Business Owner | Ambition +10, Risk Appetite +5 |
| Profession = Government Employee | Authority Respect +8, Conscientiousness +5 |
| Education > 70 | Corruption Tolerance -10, Integrity +5 |
| Income < 25 (Poor) | Empathy +5 (community solidarity) |

> **Example persona fragment:** *"… integrity 52 (moderately honest), risk appetite 65 (comfortable with disruption), empathy 58, authority respect 45 (slightly skeptical of government)."*

### 3.3 Capability Traits

Every citizen has these, but they **only matter mechanically when the citizen holds office** (minister, opposition leader). For regular citizens, these are dormant — they exist so any citizen CAN be promoted to minister without needing to generate new attributes.

| Trait | Range | Derived From | Mechanical Effect When Active |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Competence** | 0–100 | `competence_mean ± σ`, education-correlated | Raw execution ability |
| **Managerial Skill** | 0–100 | Competence × 0.6 + Conscientiousness × 0.4 ± noise | Organizing teams, managing budgets, hitting deadlines |
| **Strategic Thinking** | 0–100 | Competence × 0.5 + Education × 0.3 + Ambition × 0.2 ± noise | Quality of policy advice to the mayor |
| **Crisis Handling** | 0–100 | Competence × 0.4 + Risk Appetite × 0.3 + Conscientiousness × 0.3 ± noise | Performance during active crises in their portfolio |
| **Bureaucratic Navigation** | 0–100 | Authority Respect × 0.4 + Competence × 0.3 + Education × 0.3 ± noise | Compensates for low city admin efficiency |

> **Design note:** Capability traits are *derived* from personality + demographics with noise, not independently sampled. This means a citizen with high Competence + high Conscientiousness will naturally have high Managerial Skill — but the noise means it's not guaranteed.

### 3.4 Dynamic Wellbeing State (Primary Mutable State)

These are the citizen's **lived experience**. They shift every turn based on what the city does. These four dimensions are the **primary** mutable state on a citizen and the main input to satisfaction, alignment, and voting.

> **Note:** Wellbeing is not the *only* mutable state. Demographic traits like **income**, **education level**, and **employment status** can also shift over time — through sustained economic policies, education investment, or crisis fallout. However, these shifts are **slow and event-driven** (e.g., a prolonged economic boom gradually lifts income brackets; a collapsed school system degrades education over multiple turns). They do NOT change every turn the way wellbeing does. Think of wellbeing as the fast-moving signal and demographics as the slow-moving structural floor beneath it.

| Dimension | Range | What It Captures |
|---|---|---|
| **Health** | 0–100 | Physical wellbeing, disease exposure, access to healthcare, air quality exposure |
| **Wealth** | 0–100 | Economic security, employment stability, purchasing power, housing quality |
| **Safety** | 0–100 | Physical security, crime exposure, legal protection, emergency service access |
| **Social Belonging** | 0–100 | Community bonds, cultural inclusion, access to public spaces, democratic participation |

**Initial wellbeing** is generated from city parameters at spawn time:

```
InitialHealth  = (P4 × 0.50 + P3 × 0.20 + P10 × 0.10 + P6 × 0.10 + P2 × 0.10) × IdentityModifiers ± noise
InitialWealth  = (P1 × 0.80 + P5 × 0.10 + P6 × 0.10) × IdentityModifiers ± noise
InitialSafety  = (P8 × 0.40 + P9 × 0.20 + P6 × 0.20 + P2 × 0.10 + P3 × 0.10) × IdentityModifiers ± noise
InitialSocial  = (P7 × 0.40 + P5 × 0.20 + P4 × 0.10 + P2 × 0.10 + P9 × 0.10 + P6 × 0.10) × IdentityModifiers ± noise
```

> **v3 fix:** InitialSocial now includes P6 × 0.10 to match the Impact Matrix (previously summed to 0.90).

**Mayor Alignment** initial value:

```
InitialMayorAlignment = clamp(
    (InitialWellbeingScore - 40) × 1.2     ← citizens in a well-off city start slightly favorable
  + (AuthorityRespect - 50) × 0.3          ← respect for authority = benefit of the doubt
  + PRNG.uniform(-10, +10),                ← individual variance
  -50, +50
)

Where: InitialWellbeingScore = Health × 0.25 + Wealth × 0.30 + Safety × 0.25 + Social × 0.20
```

> The mayor starts with a honeymoon advantage in prosperous cities but NOT an overwhelming one. No citizen starts at ±100.

### 3.5 No Separate Political State — Everything Is Derived

There is no stored political satisfaction, engagement, or alignment on the citizen. These are **computed on-the-fly** each turn from the citizen's traits, wellbeing trajectory, and media environment.

| Derived Metric | Computation (detailed in Section 9) | Why Not Stored? |
|---|---|---|
| **Political Engagement** | `f(education, income, location, age, media_freedom)` | It's a function of fixed traits + a city parameter — it doesn't change turn-to-turn unless media_freedom changes |
| **Wellbeing Satisfaction** | `ΔWellbeingScore × PersonalityModifiers` | Freshly computed from current vs previous wellbeing |
| **Mayor Alignment** | *Exception: this IS stored* (see below) | — |

**Mayor Alignment** is the one political value that persists as state, because it has **memory** and **stickiness**. A citizen who loved the mayor for 8 turns and had one bad turn shouldn't immediately flip. Alignment drifts based on satisfaction but is smoothed with momentum.

```
Citizen mutable state:
  Fast (every turn)  = { Health, Wealth, Safety, Social, MayorAlignment }
  Slow (event-driven) = { Income, Education, EmploymentStatus }
  Fixed at generation = { Personality traits, Identity, Location, Age, Religion, Caste, Language }

Derived (not stored) = Political Engagement, Wellbeing Satisfaction, Vote Intent
```

### 3.6 Population Weight

Each citizen-agent represents a fraction of the total population. Weight is assigned at generation to match demographic distributions:

```
PopulationWeight(citizen) = (
    AgeBracketPercent / AgeBracketAgentCount
  + ReligionPercent  / ReligionAgentCount
  + ProfessionPercent / ProfessionAgentCount
) / 3

# Then normalize: all PopulationWeights sum to 1.0
PopulationWeight(citizen) /= Σ PopulationWeight(all citizens)
```

> With 50 agents, each has ~0.02 weight. Demographic groups with fewer representatives get proportionally higher per-agent weight, ensuring minority demographics are not under-counted in approval calculations.

## 4. The Minister-Advisor

A minister is a citizen who has been elevated to office. They do not gain new traits — the game starts *caring* about their latent capability traits.

### 4.1 What Makes a Minister

```
Minister = Citizen + Portfolio Assignment + Minister-Specific Dynamic State
```

The minister retains 100% of their citizen attributes. Portfolio assignment activates their capability traits mechanically.

### 4.2 Portfolio System (Complete MECE Mapping)

Every one of the 13 city parameters maps to exactly one portfolio. Every portfolio controls 1–3 parameters.

| Portfolio | Controls | City Parameters |
|---|---|---|
| **Finance & Economy** | Economic development, job creation | P1 (Jobs & Commerce) |
| **Infrastructure** | Transport, utilities | P2 (Transit & Roads), P3 (Water/Power/Sanitation) |
| **Health & Education** | Human development services | P4 (Hospitals), P5 (Schools) |
| **Housing & Community** | Social infrastructure | P6 (Affordable Housing), P7 (Community Spaces) |
| **Home Affairs** | Law enforcement, justice | P8 (Police), P9 (Courts) |
| **Environment** | Pollution control, sustainability | P10 (Air Quality) |
| **Governance Reform** | Administrative machinery | P11 (Admin Efficiency), P12 (Anti-Corruption), P13 (Media Freedom) |

**7 portfolios for the default 5 ministers** means some ministers hold dual portfolios. The player assigns portfolios to ministers during cabinet formation and can reshuffle.

> **Dual portfolio trade-off:** A minister holding two portfolios has their `MinisterExecScore` reduced by a factor of `0.85` for each portfolio (15% attention penalty). Three portfolios: factor `0.75`. The penalty applies multiplicatively to the final execution score for policies in each portfolio.

```
DualPortfolioPenalty:
    2 portfolios → MinisterExecScore × 0.85
    3 portfolios → MinisterExecScore × 0.75
```

### 4.3 Minister Advice Behavior (LLM-Voiced, Trait-Shaped)

When the player consults a minister, the LLM generates advice **colored by the minister's personality traits**. The math doesn't decide what the minister recommends — the LLM does — but the persona prompt ensures their character bleeds through.

| Minister Trait | Effect on Advice Pattern |
|---|---|
| High Ambition | Recommends big, visible, expensive projects (even if smaller ones are better) |
| Low Integrity | Never suggests anti-corruption reforms. Steers toward high-budget policies (more to siphon). |
| High Empathy | Focuses on vulnerable populations, social welfare |
| High Strategic Thinking | Genuinely insightful analysis (but may still be self-serving) |
| Low Competence | Vague, poorly structured recommendations |
| High Conscientiousness | Thorough, considers implementation details |
| Low Corruption Tolerance | May volunteer transparency mechanisms or suggest oversight |

> **Example:** A minister with Integrity=35, Ambition=70, Competence=63 advises: *"Sir, launch an Industrial Growth Corridor. ₹200 Cr budget. Big jobs impact."* She's right that it helps — but she's also positioning to control a large budget with her low integrity.

### 4.4 Minister Dynamic State

These are tracked per-minister in addition to their citizen state:

| Attribute | Range | Initial | What Drives Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Loyalty to Mayor** | 0–100 | 60–70 | Mayor popularity, minister ambition, public criticism, investment in their portfolio |
| **Scandal Exposure** | 0–100 | 0 | Accumulates with each corrupt act. Exposed when media catches it. |
| **Political Capital** | 0–100 | 40–60 | Their public persona. High = harder to fire. Grows with successful policy delivery. |

## 5. Turn Anatomy

One turn = one policy cycle. The game runs for a configured number of turns (default: 20) with an election at a designated turn.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ TURN N                                              │
│                                                     │
│  ① BRIEFING                                        │
│     Player sees: city stats (13 params), budget,    │
│     active crises, minister loyalty dashboard,      │
│     previous turn's delivery scorecard.             │
│     Ward Report: demographic pulse showing          │
│     which income/location/religion groups are       │
│     trending up or down (aggregate, not individual).│
│                                                     │
│  ② MINISTER CONSULTATION                           │
│     Player chats with minister-advisors (LLM).      │
│     Each advisor's recommendations reflect their    │
│     personality (ambition, integrity, empathy).     │
│     Player can ask follow-ups, challenge advice,    │
│     or request alternatives.                        │
│                                                     │
│  ③ POLICY SELECTION (Major + Minor Action)          │
│     MAJOR: Player picks ONE major policy.           │
│     MINOR: Player picks ONE minor action.           │
│     (See Section 5.3 for action types.)             │
│     System assigns the responsible minister         │
│     (based on portfolio mapping).                   │
│                                                     │
│  ④ IMPLEMENTATION ENGINE (Section 6)               │
│     Actual = Intended × MinisterExec × CityFilter  │
│     Side effects computed.                          │
│     Corruption leakage calculated.                  │
│     Implementation targets tracked.                 │
│     Budget consumed (including leakage).            │
│                                                     │

### 5.1 Ward Report (Player Information)

Each turn, the player sees an aggregate demographic pulse — NOT individual citizen data:

```

    - Locations:        avg wellbeing trend

    Plus:
    - "Hotspot" flag: any group whose avg wellbeing dropped > 5 points this turn
    - "Bright spot" flag: any group whose avg wellbeing rose > 5 points this turn

The player sees directional signals, NOT exact numbers. This provides enough information to make informed decisions without removing uncertainty.

### 5.2 Policy Generation and Validation

Policies are generated by the LLM each turn, using the city context:

```
LLM Input:
    - Current 13 city parameters
    - Budget state (treasury, debt, revenue)
    - Active crises and events
    - Minister portfolios and visible traits
    - Policy history (last 5 turns)
    - Player guidance (optional free-text from player)

LLM Output:
    5 policy options, each a DynamicPolicy struct
```

**DynamicPolicy Schema:**

```jsonc
{
    "name": "Metro Line Extension",
    "description": "Extend metro to peri-urban ring...",
    "portfolio": "Infrastructure",           // must match a valid portfolio
    "budget_cost": 250,                      // in crores, <= max_policy_budget
    "target_effects": {
        "transit_and_roads": +8,             // each effect clamped to ±10
        "air_quality_and_pollution": +2
    },
    "side_effects": {                        // mandatory: LLM must declare trade-offs
        "affordable_housing": -2,            // construction displacement
        "community_and_spaces": -1           // green space consumed
    },
    "time_profile": { "turn_0": 0.40, "turn_1": 0.35, "turn_2": 0.25 },
    "targets": [
        { "key": "km_built", "label": "Metro km Built", "unit": "km", "proposed": 12, "difficulty": 0.30 }
    ],
    "tradeoffs": "Short-term displacement of hawkers along route...",
    "why_now": "Traffic congestion at all-time worst..."
}
```

**Validation Rules (enforced programmatically after LLM generation):**

| Rule | Check |
|---|---|
| `budget_cost` ≤ `max_policy_budget` | Budget ceiling |
| Each `target_effects` value in [-10, +10] | Magnitude cap |
| Each `side_effects` value in [-5, +5] | Side effect cap |
| `time_profile` values sum to 1.0 | Profile completeness |
| `portfolio` matches a valid portfolio name | Portfolio validation |
| At least 1 `target_effects` entry | Non-empty policy |
| All targeted parameters belong to the declared portfolio | Portfolio scope check |
| `side_effects` cannot target the same params as `target_effects` | No self-overlap |

If a generated policy fails validation, it is re-prompted or replaced with a fallback from a static policy catalog.

### 5.3 Major and Minor Actions

Each turn, the player takes TWO actions:

**Major Action** (1 per turn): A full policy from the 5 LLM-generated options. This is the primary lever — substantial budget, meaningful impact, goes through the full Implementation Engine.

**Minor Action** (1 per turn): A smaller, focused action from a fixed menu:

| Minor Action | Budget | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Sector Maintenance** | 50 Cr | Pick 1 parameter: prevents decay this turn AND next turn. No positive delta. |
| **Budget Banking** | 0 | Skip minor action. Treasury gains 20 Cr bonus (efficient administration savings). |
| **Cabinet Reshuffle** | 0 | Reassign one portfolio between ministers, OR fire/hire one minister. No execution penalty this turn (transition penalty deferred to next turn). |
| **Public Address** | 30 Cr | Pick a demographic group (income/religion/location): +3 to MayorAlignment for that group's citizens. One-time, no cumulative stacking (cooldown: 3 turns). |
| **Emergency Fund** | variable | Allocate up to 100 Cr to an active crisis: CrisisResponse gets +0.15 boost this turn. |
| **Governance Upkeep** | 60 Cr | Apply +1.0 to one of P11/P12/P13 (bypasses Implementation Engine — direct maintenance). |

> **v3 rationale:** The minor action slot solves the "governance investment is suicidal" problem. Players can maintain governance parameters via minor actions while using their major policy on visible citizen-facing improvements. It also provides a "do nothing big" option via Budget Banking.

## 6. The Implementation Engine (The Gap)

Every major policy passes through two filters before it reaches the city.

### 6.1 Filter A — Minister Execution Capacity

The minister assigned to the policy's portfolio determines how much of the policy's intent actually gets organized and delivered.

```
MinisterExecScore = (
    Competence          × 0.35
  + ManagSkill          × 0.30
  + Conscientiousness   × 0.20
  + BureaucraticNav     × 0.15
) / 100

# Crisis bonus: if an active crisis exists in this minister's portfolio,
# their CrisisHandling trait provides an additive boost
If active crisis in portfolio:
    CrisisBonus = CrisisHandling / 100 × 0.10
    MinisterExecScore = min(MinisterExecScore + CrisisBonus, 1.0)

Range: 0.0 – 1.0 (typical: 0.35–0.70)
```

> **v3 fix:** Base weights now sum to 1.0 (0.35+0.30+0.20+0.15 = 1.00). CrisisHandling is an additive bonus during crises rather than a conditional weight substitution. This eliminates the 10% nerf that affected all ministers during peacetime in v2.

### 6.2 Filter B — City Institutional Filter

The city's structural capacity determines how much of the organized effort makes it through the bureaucratic pipeline.

```
CityFilterScore = (
    P11(AdminEfficiency)  × 0.40
  + P12(AntiCorruption)   × 0.30
  + InstitutionalBase     × 0.30
) / 100

Where:
  InstitutionalBase = (P9(Courts) × 0.40 + P5(Schools) × 0.35 + P8(Police) × 0.25) / 100 × 100

Range: 0.0 – 1.0 (strong city: 0.6–0.8; weak city: 0.25–0.40)
```

### 6.3 Combined Execution Score

```
RawExecution = (MinisterExecScore × 0.55) + (CityFilterScore × 0.45)

# Apply dual-portfolio penalty if applicable
RawExecution *= DualPortfolioPenalty      # 1.0, 0.85, or 0.75

Noise = PRNG.uniform(-0.05, +0.05)

ExecutionScore = clamp(RawExecution + Noise, 0.10, 0.95)
```

The minister matters slightly more (55%) because they are the direct agent. Even a brilliant minister in a broken city can't exceed ~0.65.

### 6.4 Computing Actual Impact

For each city parameter targeted by the policy:

```
If IntendedDelta > 0:
    ActualDelta = IntendedDelta × ExecutionScore       ← partial delivery

If IntendedDelta < 0:
    ActualDelta = IntendedDelta                        ← full damage always
```

**Asymmetric risk:** A poorly executed policy gives you partial benefits but full costs. Pollution from construction happens regardless of whether the road gets built.

### 6.5 Time Lag Profiles

Policies can declare a time profile for phased delivery:

```
time_profile: { "turn_0": 0.40, "turn_1": 0.35, "turn_2": 0.25 }
```

This means 40% of the actual delta is applied this turn, 35% next turn, 25% the turn after. Long-term investments (education, institutions) should use slow profiles. Quick wins (police deployment) can be immediate.

> **Carry-over counting:** A parameter receiving carry-over delta from a time-lagged policy counts as "targeted" for purposes of maintenance decay (Section 14.3). The city is still actively receiving investment — decay does not apply.

### 6.6 Implementation Target Tracking

Every policy declares concrete measurable targets:

```yaml
targets:
  - key: "jobs_supported"
    label: "New Jobs Created"
    unit: "jobs"
    proposed: 500
    difficulty: 0.20          # higher = harder to achieve

  - key: "infrastructure_km"
    label: "Road Built"
    unit: "km"
    proposed: 12
    difficulty: 0.30
```

Delivery computed per target:

```
CompletionRatio = clamp(ExecutionScore - difficulty + PRNG.uniform(-0.05, 0.07), 0.05, 1.10)
Delivered = Proposed × CompletionRatio
```

This generates the "promise vs reality" scorecard the player sees in the Turn Report.

### 6.7 Side Effects

Every policy declares `side_effects` alongside `target_effects`. Side effects are parameter changes that result from the policy's implementation regardless of execution quality.

```
For each side_effect parameter:
    # Side effects are NOT filtered by ExecutionScore.
    # They happen because of the construction/disruption itself.
    If SideEffectDelta < 0:
        ActualSideEffect = SideEffectDelta                    ← full negative impact
    If SideEffectDelta > 0:
        ActualSideEffect = SideEffectDelta × ExecutionScore   ← positive spillovers are execution-dependent
```

> **Design note:** Negative side effects always land at full strength (construction noise, displacement, pollution from roadwork). Positive spillovers (new jobs from a hospital build) are execution-dependent. This creates asymmetric risk in side effects too.

**Common side effect patterns (enforced by LLM prompt):**

| Policy Domain | Typical Negative Side Effects | Typical Positive Side Effects |
|---|---|---|
| Transit/Roads (P2) | P10 (air quality) -1 to -3, P6 (housing) -1 to -2 | P1 (jobs) +1 to +2 |
| Hospitals (P4) | P1 (jobs, budget diversion) -1 | P7 (community) +1 |
| Jobs/Commerce (P1) | P10 (pollution) -1 to -2 | P2 (transit demand) +1 |
| Housing (P6) | P7 (community disruption) -1 | P4 (health, sanitation) +1 |
| Police (P8) | P7 (community tension from policing) -1 | P9 (legal system load) -1 |

## 7. Corruption Mechanics (The Immune System)

Corruption is a deterministic consequence of character meeting opportunity.

### 7.1 Corruption Intent (Minister Side)

```
CorruptionIntent = (100 - MinisterIntegrity) / 100 × 0.40

Examples:
  Integrity 35 → Intent 0.26 (eager to siphon)
  Integrity 80 → Intent 0.08 (mostly clean)
```

### 7.2 Corruption Window (City Side)

The city's parameters act as an immune system. All references here are to the 13 city parameters.

```
CorruptionWindow = (
    (100 - P8  [Police & Emergency]) × 0.20    ← weak enforcement
  + (100 - P9  [Courts & Legal])     × 0.20    ← weak judicial accountability
  + (100 - P11 [Admin Efficiency])   × 0.15    ← opaque systems, easy to hide
  + (100 - P12 [Anti-Corruption])    × 0.25    ← no audit trail
  + (100 - P13 [Media Freedom])      × 0.20    ← no investigative journalism
) / 100

Range: 0.0–1.0. A corrupt, lawless city with no media: ~0.70. A well-governed city: ~0.20.
```

> **v3 fix:** Removed the duplicate `P12_inv × 0.10` term from v2 that double-counted anti-corruption. Redistributed its weight to Courts (0.15 → 0.20) and Media Freedom (0.15 → 0.20), which better reflects that judicial accountability and investigative journalism are independent corruption-fighting mechanisms.

### 7.3 Budget Leakage

```
BudgetFactor = min(1.0, PolicyBudget / MaxPolicyBudget)

LeakageRate = CorruptionIntent × CorruptionWindow × BudgetFactor

BudgetStolen = PolicyBudget × LeakageRate × 0.15
    # 0.15 cap ensures no single act bankrupts the city
```

### 7.4 Corruption Consequences (Every Act, Every Turn)

| What Happens | Formula | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **P12 (Anti-Corruption) damaged** | `P12 -= BudgetStolen / BaseBudget × 3.0` | Corruption corrodes institutional integrity |
| **P11 (Admin Efficiency) damaged** | `P11 -= BudgetStolen / BaseBudget × 1.5` | Stolen funds break processes |
| **Scandal Exposure builds** | `Minister.ScandalExposure += LeakageRate × 20` | Each act accumulates until caught |
| **Budget reduced** | `Treasury -= BudgetStolen` | Real money gone |

### 7.5 The Same Person, Different City

A minister with Integrity=35:
- Steals **~2.5×** more in a weak city (window 0.65) than a strong city (window 0.25)
- Gets **caught ~3×** faster in a city with high media freedom
- Causes **less systemic damage** in a city with strong courts (accountability constrains follow-on effects)

The mayor who invests in P8, P9, P11, P12, P13 is *shrinking the corruption window for all future turns* — the invisible but most powerful strategic lever in the game.

### 8.3 Per-Citizen Wellbeing Delta

For each wellbeing dimension W:

```
ΔW(citizen) = Σ over P1 to P10:
    CityParamDelta[Pi] × MatrixWeight[Pi][W] × IncomeMult × ProfessionMult × LocationMult × ReligionMult
```

**Example:** Ramesh (Poor, Informal Worker, Urban Core, Hindu) when P1 (Jobs) goes +5.0:

```
ΔWealth = 5.0 × 0.65 × 1.4 × 1.4 × 1.2 × 1.0 = +7.64
```

vs. Priya (Rich, Govt Employee, Suburban, Hindu) for the same +5.0 Jobs:

```
ΔWealth = 5.0 × 0.65 × 0.7 × 1.0 × 1.0 × 1.0 = +2.28
```

Ramesh feels the economic boom **3.4×** more than Priya.

### 8.4 Diminishing Returns at Wellbeing Extremes

Wellbeing dimensions are clamped to 0–100, with diminishing returns as values approach the boundaries:

```
# After computing raw ΔW:
If current W > 80 AND ΔW > 0:
    ΔW *= (100 - W) / 20          ← linear dampening: at W=90, only 50% of positive delta applies
                                    ← at W=95, only 25%

If current W < 20 AND ΔW < 0:
    ΔW *= W / 20                  ← same dampening for negative deltas at the floor
                                    ← at W=10, only 50% of negative delta applies

W_new = clamp(W + ΔW, 0, 100)
```

> **v3 addition:** This prevents runaway wellbeing accumulation and creates a "satisfaction plateau" — citizens at high wellbeing are harder to please further, while citizens at very low wellbeing are somewhat cushioned from total collapse (reflecting survival adaptations). It also makes the player's marginal investment less efficient at extremes, encouraging spread across demographics.

## 9. Political Feedback System

### 9.1 Wellbeing Satisfaction (Per Citizen, Per Turn)

```
WellbeingScore = Health × 0.25 + Wealth × 0.30 + Safety × 0.25 + Social × 0.20

ΔWellbeing = WellbeingScore_now - WellbeingScore_previous_turn
```

Personality modulates how the citizen *interprets* that change:

```
PersonalityModifier = (
    (AuthorityRespect - 50) × 0.015     ← respect cushions bad turns
  + (RiskAppetite    - 50) × 0.008      ← risk-tolerant citizens absorb disruption
  - (Integrity       - 50) × 0.012      ← high-integrity citizens hurt more by corruption scandals
  + (CorruptionTolerance - 50) × 0.005  ← desensitized citizens shrug off graft revelations
)

SatisfactionDelta = ΔWellbeing + PersonalityModifier

# Loss aversion: negative changes hurt 1.5× more than positive changes help
If SatisfactionDelta < 0:
    SatisfactionDelta *= 1.5
```

### 9.2 Political Engagement (Derived, Not Stored)

How much a citizen pays attention and how loudly they react:

```
PoliticalEngagement = clamp((
    Education           × 0.30
  + IncomePercentile    × 0.15
  + UrbanFactor         × 0.15     ← Urban Core = 80, Suburban = 60, Peri-Urban = 40, Rural = 25
  + AgeFactor           × 0.10     ← 36-65 = 70, 26-35 = 55, 65+ = 50, 18-25 = 40
  + P13(MediaFreedom)   × 0.20     ← city-level: free media raises awareness for everyone
  + AmbitionBoost       × 0.10     ← Ambition > 60 ? 70 : 40
) / 100, 0.0, 1.0)
```

> A 45-year-old rich, educated, urban business owner in a city with free media: engagement ~0.75.
> An 18-year-old poor, uneducated, rural informal worker in a city with suppressed media: engagement ~0.25.

### 9.3 Mayor Alignment (Stored, Sticky)

Alignment persists and has momentum. It represents deep-seated political leaning — not a snap reaction.

```
RawShift = SatisfactionDelta × PoliticalEngagement × 2.0

# Media narrative bias pushes alignment independent of reality
MediaPush = Σ over outlets:
    (OutletBias × OutletReach × OutletTrust) / Σ(OutletReach × OutletTrust)
    × CitizenMediaInfluence
    × 0.50

# Additive update with clamped drift — alignment is sticky
AlignmentDrift = clamp(RawShift + MediaPush, -8.0, +8.0)

MayorAlignment_new = clamp(
    MayorAlignment_prev + AlignmentDrift × 0.15,
    -100, +100
)
```

> **v3 fix:** The formula is now a proper additive-with-clamp update. The max per-turn shift is `±8.0 × 0.15 = ±1.2` points, creating genuinely sticky alignment. A citizen at +50 alignment who experiences the worst possible turn still only drops to ~+48.8. Over 10 consecutive bad turns, they'd drop to ~+38. This matches real-world political inertia — people don't easily abandon their political identity.

> **Comparison with v2:** The v2 formula `prev × 0.85 + (prev + shift) × 0.15` expanded to `prev + shift × 0.15` — it appeared to have smoothing but mathematically was just an additive update with no cap on drift magnitude. The v3 formula adds the `clamp(drift, -8, +8)` to prevent extreme single-turn swings while maintaining the additive nature.

### 9.4 Interim Approval Rating (What the Player Sees Between Turns)

This is weighted heavily toward **politically active citizens** — the vocal minority that shapes public discourse.

```
ActiveThreshold = 0.50     # citizens above this engagement are "active"

InterimApproval = Σ over citizens where Engagement > ActiveThreshold:
    ((MayorAlignment + 100) / 200) × Engagement × PopulationWeight
    ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    Σ over those citizens: Engagement × PopulationWeight
```

This number is what the player sees on their dashboard. It is the **perceived** approval — the Twitter/TV headline number.

### 9.5 LLM Polling (Reconciliation Check)

Every turn (or every N turns), a random sample of K citizens (e.g., K = 10–15) are polled via LLM:

```
For each sampled citizen:
    Construct persona prompt (demographic + personality + wellbeing state)
    Include: this turn's policy, media headlines, opposition attacks
    Ask: "As [Citizen Name], do you approve of the Mayor's governance? Why or why not?"

    Parse response for:
      - approve / disapprove / undecided
      - one-liner reaction (shown to player as "citizen voice")

LLMApproval = (approving citizens / total sampled) × 100
```

**Reconciliation logic:**

```
MathApproval = InterimApproval (from 9.4)
LLMApproval  = from polling

Divergence = abs(MathApproval - LLMApproval)

If Divergence < 15:  → "calibrated" — normal operation
If Divergence 15–30: → "drift warning" — logged; may indicate formula gaps
If Divergence > 30:  → "alert" — system should flag for review; possible formula recalibration needed
```

In normal gameplay, the LLM polling enriches narrative (those citizen one-liners appear in the UI) AND serves as a sanity check on the math model.

### 9.6 Election Day Approval (The Real Vote)

On election turn, ALL citizens vote — not just the active ones:

```
ElectionApproval = Σ over ALL citizens:
    ((MayorAlignment + 100) / 200) × PopulationWeight
    ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
    Σ over ALL citizens: PopulationWeight

LastMinuteSwing = (
    RecentWellbeingTrend × 0.35    ← avg satisfaction delta over last 3 turns
  + MediaNarrativeBias   × 0.25    ← cumulative media push
  + OppositionMomentum   × 0.25    ← opposition effectiveness trend
  + PRNG.uniform(-3, +3) × 0.15   ← undecided voter randomness
)

FinalMayorVoteShare = clamp(ElectionApproval + LastMinuteSwing, 0, 100)
```

### 9.7 The Silent Majority Dynamics

Why this two-tier system matters:

| Scenario | Interim Approval (Active Only) | Election Result (Everyone) | What Happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prosperous but unequal | 72% | 48% — **LOSES** | Rich vocal citizens loved it; silent poor majority suffered from housing crisis |
| Unpopular reforms that work | 38% | 55% — **WINS** | Vocal citizens hated the disruption; the silent majority noticed their water now works |
| Populist who ignores governance | 68% | 52% — barely survives | Active citizens cheered the handouts; silent majority sensed nothing structurally changed |
| Media suppression | 75% | 40% — **LOSES** | Controlled media made everything look great; citizens' actual experience told a different story |

This is the "Brexit/Trump-2016" dynamic. The information environment the player lives in (interim approval) can be deeply misleading.

## 10. The Media Engine

### 10.1 Media Landscape (From City Profile)

Each city has 3–5 outlets defined in the profile (Section 1.2). Each has:

| Attribute | Range | What It Does |
|---|---|---|
| `lean` | mayor / opposition / neutral | Editorial bias direction |
| `bias_intensity` | 0–100 | How much reporting distorts facts |
| `sensationalism` | 0–100 | Tendency to amplify negative events and drama |
| `trust_rating` | 0–100 | Citizen credibility of this outlet |
| `reach` | 0–100 | Population fraction that consumes this outlet |

**How Media Freedom (P13) shapes the landscape:**

| P13 Level | Landscape Character |
|---|---|
| P13 > 70 | Diverse, competitive press. Moderate bias. High trust. Corruption gets exposed fast. |
| P13 = 40–70 | Mixed. Some free outlets, some captured. Moderate scandal detection. |
| P13 < 30 | One dominant state-controlled outlet (bias 80+, reach 70+). Opposition outlets are underground (low reach, low trust). Scandals rarely surface. |

### 10.2 Narrative Generation (Per Turn, Per Outlet)

Each outlet generates a narrative envelope:

```
For each outlet:
    # What actually happened this turn
    RawFacts = { stat_changes, policy_enacted, events_triggered, delivery_scorecard }

    # Bias filter: shift perceived performance
    PerceivedPerformance = ActualPerformance + (BiasIntensity / 100 × LeanSign × 8.0)
        # LeanSign: +1 for mayor-lean, -1 for opposition-lean, 0 for neutral

    # Sensationalism: amplify crises, dampen good news
    If crisis_active:
        NarrativeDrama = CrisisImpact × (1.0 + Sensationalism / 100)
    Else:
        NarrativeDrama = PolicyImpact × (1.0 - Sensationalism / 300)

    # The outlet's "story" — this shapes the LLM prompt for headline generation
    NarrativeEnvelope = {
        perceived_performance,
        narrative_drama,
        lean,
        trust_rating,
        reach
    }
```

The LLM uses this envelope to generate actual headlines: *"Metro expansion shows city's commitment to growth"* (mayor-lean) vs. *"Why is half the metro budget unaccounted for?"* (opposition-lean) — same policy, different outlets.

### 10.3 Media Influence on Citizens

How much a citizen's perception is shaped by media vs. their own experience:

```
CitizenMediaExposure = (
    P13(MediaFreedom) / 100                      ← base information availability
  × LocationMediaAccess                           ← Urban=1.0, Suburban=0.85, Peri-Urban=0.70, Rural=0.55
  × (0.5 + Education / 200)                       ← education increases media consumption
)

CriticalThinking = Education / 150                 ← higher education = harder to mislead

MediaInfluence = clamp(CitizenMediaExposure × (1 - CriticalThinking), 0.05, 0.80)
```

The citizen's alignment shift for the turn blends actual experience with media narrative:

```
PerceivedSatisfaction = SatisfactionDelta × (1 - MediaInfluence) + MediaNarrativeDelta × MediaInfluence
```

> A well-educated citizen in a city with free media: MediaInfluence ~0.25 (mostly trusts own experience).
> An uneducated citizen in a controlled media city: MediaInfluence ~0.70 (believes the headlines).

### 10.4 Scandal Exposure Mechanic

When a minister's `ScandalExposure` accumulates past a threshold, the media may break the story:

```
For each minister with ScandalExposure > 20:

    ScandalBreakProbability = (
        ScandalExposure / 100
      × P13(MediaFreedom) / 100
      × HasInvestigativeOutlet               ← 1.0 if any outlet has trust > 50 AND lean ≠ mayor; else 0.3
      × (1.0 + OppositionPressure × 0.30)   ← opposition can amplify scandal signals
    )

    If PRNG.random() < ScandalBreakProbability:
        SCANDAL BREAKS:
            → Minister.Loyalty -= 15 to 25
            → Minister.PoliticalCapital -= 10 to 20
            → MayorAlignment shifts negative for HIGH-INTEGRITY citizens
            → P12(AntiCorruption) takes a small hit (institutional embarrassment)
            → Player must decide: fire minister / deny / launch investigation
```

**In a high-media-freedom city:** a minister with ScandalExposure=40 has ~30% chance of being caught per turn. Corruption is risky.

**In a low-media-freedom city:** the same minister might only have ~5% chance. They can steal for 10+ turns before getting caught — but when the dam breaks (as it always eventually does), the accumulated exposure makes the scandal massive.

### 10.5 Media as a Strategic Lever

The mayor can *target* P13 (Media Freedom) with policy:

| If mayor INCREASES P13 | Effects |
|---|---|
| Scandal detection rises | Corrupt ministers get caught faster |
| Citizen engagement rises system-wide | More people pay attention (approval becomes more volatile) |
| Media bias diversity increases | Less state-captured narrative |
| Opposition gets more airtime | Their attacks land harder too |

| If mayor DECREASES P13 (press suppression) | Effects |
|---|---|
| Scandal detection drops | Can hide ministerial corruption longer |
| Citizen engagement drops | Fewer people paying attention (approval stabilizes but decouples from reality) |
| State-controlled narrative | Pro-mayor bias dominates… for now |
| **BUT: Corruption window widens** | Loss of the watchdog effect means MORE leakage |
| **AND: Election surprise risk** | Interim approval looks great; actual voting population may disagree |

Suppressing media is tempting — it makes the dashboard look good. But it makes the city's immune system weaker and the election increasingly unpredictable.

### 10.6 Media Drift

Media outlets are NOT static. Their attributes shift based on city conditions and policy history:

```
Each turn, for each outlet:

    # Trust erodes when bias is extreme and media freedom is high (fact-checking environment)
    If P13 > 60 AND outlet.bias_intensity > 60:
        outlet.trust_rating -= 0.5 per turn     ← audiences notice distortion in a free press

    # Trust recovers when bias moderates
    If outlet.bias_intensity < 40:
        outlet.trust_rating += 0.3 per turn (capped at initial_trust + 10)

    # Lean can shift toward mayor when mayor is very popular
    If InterimApproval > 70 AND outlet.lean == "neutral":
        10% chance per turn: outlet.lean → "mayor" (bandwagon effect)

    # Lean can shift toward opposition when mayor is very unpopular
    If InterimApproval < 30 AND outlet.lean == "neutral":
        10% chance per turn: outlet.lean → "opposition"

    # Media suppression effect: when P13 drops sharply
    If ΔP13 < -5 this turn:
        All opposition-lean outlets: reach -= 5 (capped at min 10)
        All mayor-lean outlets: reach += 3 (capped at initial_reach + 15)

    # Reach recovery when media freedom rises
    If ΔP13 > +3 this turn:
        All outlets: reach drifts 1.0 toward initial value per turn
```

> **v3 addition:** This makes media a living system. Sustained press suppression doesn't just change a number — it visibly transforms which outlets citizens hear from. A neutral outlet that leans toward the mayor after sustained popularity can be a trap: if popularity drops, it takes time for the outlet to shift back, creating a lagging narrative effect.

## 11. The Opposition Engine

### 11.1 Opposition Leader Profile

Generated from the citizen pool: high Ambition (70+), moderate-to-high Competence, variable Integrity.

| Leader Trait | Strategic Effect |
|---|---|
| High Ambition + High Integrity | Principled critic — attacks genuine failures. High credibility. Hard to dismiss. |
| High Ambition + Low Integrity | Demagogue — uses misinformation, identity politics, manufactured outrage. Effective short-term, but credibility erodes if fact-checked. |
| High Competence | Identifies the mayor's actual weak points. Attacks land harder. |
| Low Competence | Attacks are unfocused. Misidentifies the vulnerable front. |

### 11.2 Opposition Turn Behavior

Each turn, the opposition evaluates the city and picks an **attack strategy**:

```
For each city parameter Pi:
    VulnerabilityScore = max(0, 60 - Pi) × RecentDecline × PublicSalience

    Where:
        RecentDecline = max(1.0, 1.0 + (Pi_prev - Pi_now) × 0.2)   ← recent drops amplify vulnerability
        PublicSalience = row_sum of Pi in Impact Matrix               ← high-impact params are more salient

AttackFront = argmax(VulnerabilityScore)
```

| Strategy | Trigger Condition | Effect on Citizens |
|---|---|---|
| **Delivery Failure** | ExecutionScore was low this turn | Amplifies the gap. "They promised 500 jobs — only 250 materialized!" |
| **Corruption Accusation** | Scandal broke or high ScandalExposure + opposition lean outlets | Credibility damage. Most effective on high-integrity citizens. |
| **Identity Mobilization** | A demographic segment got negative wellbeing delta | Targets that group specifically. "The housing demolitions hurt YOUR community!" |
| **Crisis Blame** | Active crisis event | Amplifies the crisis, blames the mayor, proposes (impractical) alternatives. |
| **Populist Promise** | Election approaching (within 3 turns) | Unrealistic benefits promised. Pulls low-information, low-engagement voters. |

### 11.3 Opposition Effectiveness

```
OppositionEffectiveness = (
    LeaderCompetence / 100   × 0.30
  + AttackCredibility        × 0.30    ← is the attack grounded in real data?
  + MediaAmplification       × 0.25    ← do opposition-lean outlets amplify it?
  + PublicFrustration        × 0.15    ← inversely correlated with avg wellbeing
)

AttackCredibility:
    If attack is based on real failure (low ExecutionScore, actual scandal):
        Credibility = 0.80 to 1.0
    If fabricated (opposition integrity low, no evidence):
        Credibility = 0.20 to 0.40
        AND: if MediaFreedom high + investigative outlets exist → credibility drops further (fact-checked)
```

### 11.4 Counter-Frame (Player Choice)

The player picks a defensive strategy. Each has a formula for effectiveness:

| Counter-Frame | Best When | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Delivery Receipts** | You actually delivered well | Shows measurable results. Effective on engaged citizens. |
| **Empathy + Relief** | A group was genuinely hurt | Acknowledge pain, offer immediate relief. Effective on high-empathy citizens. |
| **Accountability Pivot** | Corruption accusation is real | Launch investigation, increase transparency. Risky but credible. |
| **Attack the Attacker** | Opposition is dishonest | Call out misinformation. Only works if media freedom is high enough to verify. |
| **Populist Counter** | Short-term survival needed | Promise something big. Buys time but increases credibility liability. |

**Counter-Frame Effectiveness Formula:**

```
CounterEffectiveness = BaseEffect × ContextMatch × MediaAmplification

Where:
    BaseEffect (per counter-frame):
        Delivery Receipts:    0.60
        Empathy + Relief:     0.55
        Accountability Pivot: 0.70
        Attack the Attacker:  0.50
        Populist Counter:     0.65

    ContextMatch (0.3 – 1.5, measures how well the counter fits the situation):
        Delivery Receipts:    min(1.5, AvgExecutionScore × 1.5)     ← only works if you actually delivered
        Empathy + Relief:     1.0 + (60 - AvgWellbeingOfTargetGroup) / 100   ← better when group is genuinely hurting
        Accountability Pivot: 1.0 + (P12 / 100) × 0.5              ← needs institutional backing to be credible
        Attack the Attacker:  (100 - OppositionIntegrity) / 100 × P13 / 100  ← only if opponent is lying AND press is free
        Populist Counter:     1.2 - (TurnsSinceLastPopulistCounter × 0.15)  ← diminishing returns with repetition

    MediaAmplification:
        = Σ over mayor-lean outlets: (OutletReach × OutletTrust) / Σ(AllOutletReach × AllOutletTrust)

NetOppositionImpact = OppositionEffectiveness × (1.0 - CounterEffectiveness)
    Clamped to [0.0, 1.0]

# Apply to citizen alignment
For each citizen:
    OppositionAlignmentPush = NetOppositionImpact × PoliticalEngagement × -2.0
    # This feeds into the MayorAlignment drift as an additional negative term
```

> **v3 addition:** Counter-frames now have real math. "Delivery Receipts" is strong only if your ExecutionScore is genuinely high. "Attack the Attacker" requires both a dishonest opponent AND a free press. "Populist Counter" has built-in diminishing returns — you can't keep making empty promises.

### 11.5 Opposition Evolution

The opposition leader is NOT static. Their political standing evolves based on attack success:

```
OppositionCredibility (0–100, starts at 50):

Each turn:
    If attack was fact-based AND landed (NetOppositionImpact > 0.4):
        OppositionCredibility += 3
    If attack was fabricated AND got fact-checked (MediaFreedom > 60, low AttackCredibility):
        OppositionCredibility -= 5
    If attack was irrelevant (targeted a non-vulnerable front):
        OppositionCredibility -= 2

Effects of OppositionCredibility:
    - Modifies OppositionEffectiveness: × (OppositionCredibility / 50)
    - At OppositionCredibility < 20: opposition enters "crisis of confidence"
        → 20% chance per turn: opposition leader replaced by another high-ambition citizen
        → Replacement starts at OppositionCredibility = 40 (fresh start, partial reset)
    - At OppositionCredibility > 80: opposition enters "momentum mode"
        → +5% to all attack effectiveness
        → Election LastMinuteSwing gets OppositionMomentum × 0.5 bonus
```

> **v3 addition:** The opposition now has its own trajectory. A competent, principled opposition leader becomes increasingly dangerous over time. A demagogue in a city with free press self-destructs. The player can strategically undermine opposition credibility by maintaining high MediaFreedom (which fact-checks fabricated attacks) or by delivering well (which makes "Delivery Failure" attacks non-credible).

When city parameters cross critical thresholds, events become inevitable:

| P8 (Police) < 30 | Crime wave | "Serial mugging spree in ward 12" |
| P7 (Community) < 20 AND CommunalTension > 60 | Communal violence | "Religious procession clash turns violent" |
| P3 (Water/Sanitation) < 20 | Disease outbreak | "Cholera cases spike in eastern slums" |

> **v3 fix:** Communal violence trigger now uses `CommunalTension` (Section 12.5) instead of undefined `income_inequality`. Opportunity events added (see Section 12.5).

### 12.2 Stochastic Events

```
BaseEventChance = 0.15

ModifiedChance = BaseEventChance + (
    max(0, 60 - AvgOfP1toP10) / 200          ← worse city = more crises
  + ActiveCrisisCount × 0.05                  ← cascading problems
  - P11(AdminEfficiency) / 400                ← good admin = preparedness
)

If PRNG.random() < ModifiedChance:
    Generate event from weighted pool matching city vulnerabilities
```

**Stochastic Opportunity Events:**

```
OpportunityChance = 0.05 + (
    max(0, AvgOfP1toP10 - 55) / 300          ← better city = more opportunities
  + P11(AdminEfficiency) / 500                ← good admin = able to capitalize
  - ActiveCrisisCount × 0.03                  ← crises suppress opportunity
)

If PRNG.random() < OpportunityChance:
    Generate opportunity from pool matching city strengths
```

### 12.3 Event Structure

```yaml
# Crisis example
event:
    name: "Monsoon Street Flooding"
    type: crisis
    severity: 1-3
    duration: 2-5 turns
    city_effects_per_turn:
        transit_and_roads: -3.0
        hospitals_and_clinics: -2.0
        community_and_spaces: +1.0     # communities rally together
    portfolio: Infrastructure
    escalation_chance: 0.25
    escalation_effects:
        transit_and_roads: -5.0
        police_and_emergency: -4.0

# Opportunity example
event:
    name: "International Tech Summit Bid"
    type: opportunity
    severity: 2
    duration: 2 turns
    window: 1 turn                     # player must act within 1 turn to capitalize
    opportunity_effects:               # applied ONLY if player invests (via minor action Emergency Fund)
        jobs_and_commerce: +4.0
        transit_and_roads: +2.0
        media_freedom: +1.0            # international attention
    miss_effects:                      # applied if player doesn't capitalize
        jobs_and_commerce: -1.0        # "city passed over" narrative
    portfolio: Finance & Economy
```

> **v3 addition:** Opportunity events have a `window` — the player has limited turns to invest in capitalizing on them. Missing the window has a small negative consequence (narrative damage). This creates a new decision dimension: spend limited resources on an opportunity, or stick to your planned investment?

### 12.4 Crisis Response Quality

```
CrisisResponse = MinisterCrisisHandling × 0.60 + P11(AdminEfficiency) / 100 × 0.40

If CrisisResponse > 0.60:
    Duration shortened 1 turn, escalation chance halved, mayor +2 alignment boost

If CrisisResponse < 0.30:
    Escalation chance doubled, mayor -3 alignment penalty
```

### 12.5 Communal Tension System

Communal tension is tracked as a city-level variable that interacts with religious identity:

```
CommunalTension (0–100, starts at communal_config.tension_baseline):

Each turn:
    # Base drift toward baseline
    CommunalTension += (tension_baseline - CommunalTension) × 0.05

    # Festival calendar boosts
    If current turn is in a festival_calendar range:
        CommunalTension += festival.tension_boost

    # Policy impact
    If policy targeted P7 (Community Spaces) with positive delta:
        CommunalTension -= ActualDelta × 0.8    ← community investment eases tension
    If policy targeted P8 (Police) with positive delta AND minority religion sensitivity for P8 > 1.2:
        CommunalTension += ActualDelta × 0.3    ← heavy policing can inflame minority communities

    # Event cascades
    If active crisis has communal dimension:
        CommunalTension += crisis.severity × 3.0

    # Opposition identity mobilization
    If opposition strategy == "Identity Mobilization":
        CommunalTension += OppositionEffectiveness × 5.0

    CommunalTension = clamp(CommunalTension, 0, 100)
```

**Effects of high communal tension:**

| CommunalTension Level | Effect |
|---|---|
| < 30 | Normal. No special effects. |
| 30–60 | MayorAlignment for minority citizens: extra -1 per turn (anxiety). Community Spaces (P7) gets +0.5 maintenance decay. |
| 60–80 | Above effects, plus: stochastic communal violence chance = (Tension - 60) / 100 per turn. P8 (Police) effectiveness reduced by 10% (stretched resources). |
| > 80 | All above, plus: international media attention. P13 rises +1 per turn (scrutiny). P1 (Jobs) drops -1 per turn (investor flight). |

## 13. Cabinet Dynamics

### 13.1 Minister Loyalty Drift

```
PortfolioInvestment = (
    BudgetSpentOnThisPortfolioThisTurn / max_policy_budget × 100
  + (1 if MinorAction was SectorMaintenance targeting this portfolio else 0) × 20
)
# Range: 0–120 (typical: 0–60)

LoyaltyDelta = (
    (InterimApproval - 50)         × 0.10    ← popular mayor → loyal ministers
  - (MinisterAmbition - 50)        × 0.08    ← ambitious ministers are restless
  + PortfolioInvestment            × 0.05    ← investment in their department = appreciation
  - ScandalExposure                × 0.03    ← embarrassed ministers blame the mayor
)

Loyalty = clamp(PrevLoyalty + LoyaltyDelta, 0, 100)
```

> **v3 fix:** `PortfolioInvestment` is now defined as a 0–120 composite of budget allocation and minor action targeting. A minister whose portfolio received a 300 Cr policy (300/500 × 100 = 60) and a maintenance minor action (+20) gets `PortfolioInvestment = 80`, contributing `80 × 0.05 = +4` to loyalty.

### 13.2 Defection Risk

```
If Loyalty < 30 AND Ambition > 60:
    DefectionChance = (30 - Loyalty) / 100 × Ambition / 100 × 0.50

    If PRNG.random() < DefectionChance:
        MINISTER DEFECTS TO OPPOSITION
        → Media scandal (massive negative coverage)
        → Mayor alignment -5 to -10 across all citizens
        → Portfolio runs at 0.5× execution for 1 turn (vacancy)
        → Player must appoint replacement
```

### 13.3 Firing a Minister

```
If Minister.PoliticalCapital > 60:
    → Mayor alignment -3 (popular minister, perceived vendetta)
    → Opposition runs "instability" narrative

If Minister.PoliticalCapital < 30:
    → No political cost (expendable)

Always:
    → Portfolio at 0.5× execution for 1 turn (transition)

```

### 13.4 Appointing Ministers

The player sees 3–5 candidates from the citizen pool with **partial information**:

| Visible to Player | Hidden from Player |
|---|---|
| Age, Profession, Education, Location, Religion | Exact trait values |
| Reputation signals ("Known for efficiency", "Ambitious", "Connected to contractors") | Integrity, Corruption Tolerance |

The player is hiring under **asymmetric information**. The most competent-looking candidate might secretly be corrupt. The quiet, unremarkable one might be the most honest.

## 14. Budget & Resource System

### 14.1 Revenue Per Turn

```
TurnRevenue = BaseTaxRevenue × (P1(Jobs) / 50) × (1 - CorruptionTaxLeakage)

Where:
    CorruptionTaxLeakage = (100 - P12(AntiCorruption)) / 100 × 0.15
```

Better economy → more revenue. High corruption → tax collectors skim before it reaches the treasury.

### 14.2 Policy Cost and Debt

Each policy has a declared budget cost. If the treasury can't cover it:

1. **Use treasury** (preferred): Treasury -= PolicyBudget
2. **Take debt**: If treasury insufficient, the shortfall becomes debt.

```
Debt System:
    OutstandingDebt starts at 0
    MaxDebt = budget.max_debt (from city profile, default 2000 Cr)
    InterestRate = budget.interest_rate (default 0.08 per turn)

    Each turn (before revenue collection):
        InterestPayment = OutstandingDebt × InterestRate
        Treasury -= InterestPayment
        # Interest is paid first, before revenue is added

    When taking debt:
        If OutstandingDebt + Shortfall > MaxDebt:
            → Policy CANNOT be enacted (insufficient capacity)
            → Player must choose a cheaper policy or take a "Budget Banking" minor action
        Else:
            OutstandingDebt += Shortfall

    Paying down debt:
        If Treasury > 200 at end of turn:
            AutoRepayment = min(Treasury - 200, OutstandingDebt × 0.25)
            OutstandingDebt -= AutoRepayment
            Treasury -= AutoRepayment
```

**Political cost of debt:**

```
If OutstandingDebt > MaxDebt × 0.50:
    → "Fiscal Warning" shown in Ward Report
    → Opposition gains "Fiscal Mismanagement" attack strategy (Credibility = 0.70)
    → P11 (Admin Efficiency) -= 0.5 per turn (bureaucratic strain from debt servicing)

If OutstandingDebt > MaxDebt × 0.80:
    → "Fiscal Crisis" — all above, plus:
    → Revenue reduced by additional 10% (credit rating damage)
    → All minister loyalty -3 per turn (sinking ship effect)
```

### 14.3 Maintenance Decay

Every city parameter has a maintenance cost. If the player doesn't invest in a parameter for multiple turns, it decays:

```
For each Pi (P1 to P13):
    targeted_this_turn = (
        policy targeted Pi this turn
        OR time-lagged carry-over from a previous policy is delivering delta to Pi
        OR minor action "Sector Maintenance" targeted Pi
    )

    targeted_last_turn = same check for previous turn

    If NOT targeted_this_turn AND NOT targeted_last_turn:
        DecayRate = 1.5 × (1 - P11(AdminEfficiency) / 200)
        Pi -= DecayRate

        # Good administration slows decay (efficient maintenance)
```

> **v3 fix:** Time-lagged carry-overs now explicitly count as "targeting" a parameter. A 3-turn slow-profile policy on P5 (Schools) prevents decay for all 3 turns of delivery. Minor action "Sector Maintenance" also counts as targeting.

This prevents "set and forget" — the city needs continuous investment. Neglected sectors deteriorate.

## 15. Governance Equilibrium (The Long Game)

P11 (Admin Efficiency) doesn't exist in isolation. It trends toward a natural equilibrium determined by structural foundations.

### 15.1 Equilibrium Target

```
EquilibriumTarget = (
    P13(MediaFreedom)      × 0.30    ← watchdog forces accountability
  + P5(Schools)            × 0.30    ← educated workforce for civil service
  + P1(Jobs)               × 0.20    ← tax base funds the machinery
  + P9(Courts)             × 0.20    ← legal enforcement of accountability
)
```

### 15.2 Drift

```
DriftDirection = EquilibriumTarget - P11(AdminEfficiency)
DriftAmount = clamp(DriftDirection × 0.04, -2.0, +2.0)

P11 += DriftAmount
```

A one-off "Anti-Corruption Campaign" that pushes P11 to 90 will decay rapidly if Schools=20 and MediaFreedom=30. The foundations don't support it. But investing in Schools, Courts, and Media Freedom will cause P11 to *naturally rise* over time.

### 15.3 Media Freedom Vulnerability

```
If P8(Police) < 30:
    P13(MediaFreedom) -= 1.5 per turn     ← journalists unsafe, self-censor

If P11(AdminEfficiency) < 25:
    P13(MediaFreedom) -= 0.8 per turn     ← bureaucratic obstruction of press
```

A city that loses police control AND administrative competence will also lose its free press — the last watchdog falls, corruption window blows open, and the downward spiral accelerates.

## 16. The Election and Beyond

### 16.1 When

Fixed turn (default: turn 12 of 20). Player knows it's coming.

### 16.2 Voting

Uses the **full-population model** from Section 9.6:

```
FinalMayorVoteShare = ElectionApproval(all citizens) + LastMinuteSwing

If FinalMayorVoteShare < 50: MAYOR LOSES → Game Over
If FinalMayorVoteShare ≥ 50: Mayor survives → game continues into Legacy Phase
```

### 16.3 Margin Effects and Legacy Phase

| Result | Post-Election Effect |
|---|---|
| Landslide (>65%) | Ministers loyalty +10 all, opposition weakened, media favorable |
| Comfortable (55–65%) | Normal continuation |
| Narrow (50–55%) | Minister loyalty -5 all, opposition emboldened (+15% effectiveness), media sensationalism spikes |
| Loss (<50%) | Game Over |

**The Legacy Phase (Turns 13–20)**

After surviving the election, the game shifts from survival mode to legacy building. The player now has a **second-term mandate** with new mechanics:

```
Legacy Phase changes:
    1. LEGACY SCORECARD visible on dashboard (updated each turn):
       - Shows real-time score across all 7 governance metrics (Section 17.2)
       - Each metric shows trend arrow and delta from election day values

    2. MANDATE PRESSURE:
       - Election margin determines "political capital" for the remaining turns:
         Landslide:   Can enact 1 policy per turn with budget_cost up to 1.5× max_policy_budget
         Comfortable: Normal policy budget
         Narrow:      max_policy_budget reduced by 20% (limited mandate)

    3. LEGACY EVENTS (turn 15 and turn 18):
       - Turn 15: "Mid-Term Review" — media publishes a comprehensive governance assessment
         → If avg city params improved > 5 from start: mayor alignment +5 across all citizens
         → If avg city params declined: mayor alignment -5, opposition gets "Failed Mandate" attack
       - Turn 18: "Legacy Summit" — citizens reflect on the full arc of governance
         → LLM generates a narrative summary of the mayor's tenure
         → Citizens with wellbeing > 60: alignment +3. Citizens with wellbeing < 40: alignment -3

    4. NO SECOND ELECTION, but:
       - Loss conditions still apply (bankruptcy, governance collapse, mass despair, city in crisis)
       - If any loss condition triggers during Legacy Phase: title capped at "The Survivor" (max 69)

    5. INSTITUTIONAL MOMENTUM:
       - Governance equilibrium drift (Section 15.2) doubled during Legacy Phase
       - Long-term investments finally pay off: time-lagged policies from turns 10–12 deliver
       - Corruption consequences compound: ignored corruption accelerates
```

> **v3 addition:** The Legacy Phase gives turns 13–20 genuine stakes. The visible Legacy Scorecard creates a "score chase" dynamic. Mandate Pressure means the election margin has lasting consequences. Legacy Events at turns 15 and 18 create narrative beats that punctuate the second half. The institutional momentum doubling rewards (or punishes) long-term strategy.

### 17.2 Governance Scorecard (End of Game)

| Metric | What It Measures | Scoring (0–100) |
|---|---|---|
| **Final Approval** | How the full population feels about you | ElectionApproval formula on final turn |
| **Wellbeing Equity** | Gap between richest and poorest citizen wellbeing | `100 - (MaxWellbeing - MinWellbeing)` |
| **Institutional Legacy** | (P11+P12+P13 at end) vs (P11+P12+P13 at start) | `50 + (EndGov - StartGov) × 2` clamped 0–100 |
| **Budget Health** | Remaining treasury + debt level | `(Treasury / StartingTreasury) × 50 + (1 - Debt/MaxDebt) × 50` |
| **Crisis Record** | Events triggered, escalated, resolved | `100 - (CrisesTriggered × 5 + Escalations × 10 - Resolutions × 8)` |
| **Promise-Delivery Ratio** | Average ExecutionScore across all turns | `AvgExecutionScore × 100` |
| **Cabinet Integrity** | Total stolen, defections, scandal count | `100 - (TotalStolen/StartingTreasury × 50 + Defections × 15 + Scandals × 10)` |

**Final Score = weighted average:**
```
FinalScore = (
    FinalApproval        × 0.20
  + WellbeingEquity      × 0.15
  + InstitutionalLegacy  × 0.20
  + BudgetHealth         × 0.10
  + CrisisRecord         × 0.10
  + PromiseDelivery      × 0.10
  + CabinetIntegrity     × 0.15
)
```

### The Spirals

**Vicious:** Weak city → corrupt ministers siphon → institutions weaken → P11 drops → next policy delivers less → citizens suffer → approval drops → ministers defect → portfolio gaps → city degrades further.

**Virtuous:** Invest in governance (P5, P8, P9, P11, P12, P13) via minor actions + occasional major policy → equilibrium rises → P11 drifts up → corruption window shrinks → more policy delivered → citizens improve → approval rises → ministers stay loyal → stable governance → more investment capacity.

**The Dilemma:** Governance investment is *invisible to citizens*. Building schools doesn't make anyone immediately happy. The improvement in P11 only shows up as "your next policy delivers 60% instead of 45%." The mayor who invests long-term risks losing the election before the returns materialize. The mayor who goes for visible populism wins approval today but entrenches structural rot. **The minor action slot gives just enough room for governance maintenance without sacrificing visible policy — but not enough to fully invest. The tension remains.**

**The Information Trap:** Suppressing media (lowering P13) makes the dashboard look great — interim approval stabilizes, scandals don't surface, opposition is muffled. But the corruption window widens, the governance equilibrium target drops, citizens' *actual* wellbeing diverges from the *narrative* wellbeing… and on election day, the silent majority votes on reality, not headlines. **And now media outlets visibly shift toward state capture, making the trap even more seductive.**

**The Communal Powder Keg:** Ignoring community investment (P7) while pumping police (P8) may look like a safety play, but it inflames communal tension in minority communities. One festival, one opposition identity mobilization play, one bad turn — and the tension boils over into communal violence that damages everything.

That is the game.
